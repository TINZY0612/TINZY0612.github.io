FAQ

What is Arc?

Arc is the Economic OS for the internet: an open internet platform purpose-built for the world’s financial markets, real-time settlement, and agentic economic activity. Arc gives builders, businesses, institutions, and AI agents the open platform they need to power onchain lending, capital markets, FX, payments, treasury operations, and new forms of programmable economic activity.

At its foundation is Arc Network, an open, Layer-1 blockchain designed for reliable value exchange at internet scale. Arc combines predictable dollar-based fees, deterministic sub-second finality, a future opt-in privacy model, and a known institutional validator set with deep integration across the Circle platform.

What use cases does Arc support?

Arc supports financial applications that need real-time value movement, programmable execution, predictable costs, and reliable settlement finality.

These capabilities power a range of use cases such as:

  • Agent-executable financial workflows
  • Onchain lending and borrowing
  • Capital markets
  • FX and stablecoin exchange
  • Global payments
  • Treasury operations
  • Asset issuance and distribution
  • Tokenized funds and real-world assets

Arc is designed for builders, businesses, institutions, and AI agents that need to move, exchange, settle, and coordinate value in real time across borders, markets, counterparties, and software environments.

How does Arc support privacy and compliance?

Future opt-in privacy features on Arc will allow businesses to selectively shield sensitive transaction details while preserving auditability. This gives builders more flexibility when designing financial applications that need transparency for verification, reporting, or compliance, while also protecting information that should not be exposed publicly by default.

How does Arc support AI agents and agent-led value flows?

Arc gives AI agents stablecoin-native infrastructure for moving value, executing transactions, and coordinating economic activity in real time. Predictable low fees and deterministic sub-second finality make payments behave more like API calls: clear, fast, and settled with certainty. This can unlock high-frequency, sub-cent flows like nanopayments, enabling agents to pay, trade, retrieve data, coordinate, and do business at machine scale.

What is Arc Portal?

Arc Portal is the primary consumer entry point into the Arc ecosystem. Users can set up and fund wallets, swap assets, access earn opportunities, discover apps, and track balances and activity from one place. Arc Portal supports agent wallet provisioning, giving users a way to fund agent wallets, set spend limits, and delegate onchain tasks with visibility and control.

Who can build on Arc?

Arc is an open, enterprise-grade platform designed for anyone to build and deploy, from independent developers and startups to fintechs, institutions, and global enterprises. Builders can use Arc to create applications for payments, FX, credit, tokenized assets, treasury workflows, capital markets settlement, and emerging agentic economic activity.

How can I get started building on Arc?

Arc is an open internet platform designed for broad participation by developers, startups, enterprises, institutions, and ecosystem participants. There are several ways to engage with the Arc ecosystem:

  • Developers: To start building, visit the Arc docs, view the quickstart guides, or try building with Arc Studio.
  • Businesses and institutions: Contact your Circle rep to discuss partnership opportunities.
  • Community members: Follow @Arc on X, join the Arc Discord for roadmap updates and ecosystem news, or become involved with the Arc House, a central hub where you can attend events and connect with Arc’s global builder community.
How is Arc secured?

Arc is operated by a permissioned validator set of known operators, with network-level sanctions controls and third-party monitoring across network activity. Teams building on Arc can also access developer security resources, audit support, and the Arc Bug Bounty. Learn more about security on Arc.
