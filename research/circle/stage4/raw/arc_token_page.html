ARC: the native coordination asset of the Arc network

The ARC whitepaper explores how a network token could help coordinate the Arc network as it grows — supporting security, governance, fee mechanics, and broader utility across the network.

Read the whitepaperRead the blog

ARC is not launched. All products and features are subject to change.

Shared infrastructure needs shared alignment

Financial infrastructure is approaching the same threshold computing reached before shared operating systems: growing activity, fragmented systems, and no common environment for participants to build and operate together.

Arc provides common, internet-native rails for stablecoins, tokenized assets, financial applications, and global markets.

The ARC token would serve as a coordination layer: a protocol-native mechanism intended to align network security, economic governance, and participation within the Arc network.

Coordination assets align networks

Governance tokens help participants vote, while utility tokens help participants access networks or features. A coordination asset is different: it connects the core functions of a network into a single, reinforcing system. Security, governance, fee mechanics, and utility are not separate token features; they work together to align participants with how the network operates and evolves over time.

ARC is designed around a set of reinforcing functions

Economic governance

ARC gives participants a role in shaping key economic parameters including fees, inflation, and burn logic.

Fee mechanics

ARC connects stablecoin-based fees to reward and burn mechanisms tied to real usage.

Platform utility

ARC supports broader platform utility as Arc expands across new services, applications, and protocol capabilities.

Broader network participation

ARC supports the Arc network’s future transition from Proof-of-Authority to Proof-of-Stake through staking and delegation.

FAQ

Does the whitepaper confirm the launch of a native Arc token?

No. A native token for the Arc network is actively being explored but no final decision has been made. Any token design, functionality, timing, or rollout remains subject to change.

Has the ARC token launched?

No. The ARC token has not launched. The whitepaper outlines a proposed coordination model for the Arc network, but it does not activate a live token, staking system, or governance mechanics.

Does the ARC token represent equity in Circle?

No. ARC would not represent equity in Circle or confer any claim on Circle’s revenues, profits, assets, or CRCL shares. Its intended role is as a utility-driven coordination asset for participation in the Arc network itself.
